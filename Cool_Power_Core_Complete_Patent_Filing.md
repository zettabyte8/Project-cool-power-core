## ABSTRACT
A closed-loop thermo-chemical and electro-mechanical energy conversion system ("Cool Power Core") is disclosed. Liquid water stored in an insulated thermal reservoir is preheated to approximately 90°C using recovered exhaust and engine-block waste heat, reducing sensible heating loads by 43.93 W per 0.54 L/hr feed rate and lowering ultrasonic/vortex cavitation power requirements by 30% to 45%. Preheated water is injected into a chemical-vapor-deposition (CVD) graphene-coated fluidic vortex amplifier driven by a shaped high-temperature exhaust control stream, generating radical-rich cold steam containing ambient air or urban smog particulates. A 4.2 V, 20 W zero-voltage-switching (ZVS) nanosecond-pulsed driver coupled to a 1000× MnZn ferrite U-core transformer and an 8-stage Cockcroft-Walton multiplier applies a 33.6 kV corona/ozone discharge across the cavitated mist to crack water molecules and oxidize carbonaceous particulates. The resulting reactive charge undergoes 30:1 compression and ~3,000°C reaction within a 50 Hz opposed-piston core featuring yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramic liners, 5-micron porous air bearings, and an argon-filled silica aerogel thermal barrier protecting radial-magnetized N52H linear alternators below 100°C. Exhaust energy is further harvested via a micro Variable Geometry Turbocharger (VGT), biomimetic micro Tesla steam turbine, electrostatic ion grid, thermoelectric/pyroelectric stack, bismuth titanate piezoelectric pulse harvesters, and a V₂O₅/TiO₂ SCR honeycomb catalyst prior to exhaust condensation and closed-loop water recirculation.

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION
[0001] The present invention relates generally to closed-loop thermo-chemical energy conversion, hydrodynamic and ultrasonic cavitation, nanosecond high-voltage plasma dissociation, and free-piston linear alternator power generation, and more particularly to an integrated waste-heat-assisted water cavitation, corona plasma cracking, and multi-modal energy harvesting architecture designated as the **Cool Power Core**.

---

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION
[0002] Conventional internal combustion engines and thermal powerplants reject 55% to 70% of fuel enthalpy as low- and medium-grade waste heat via engine cooling jackets and exhaust streams. Furthermore, ambient air in urban and marine environments contains unburned hydrocarbons, volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and carbonaceous particulate matter (PM₂.₅/PM₁₀) that represent untapped chemical enthalpy as well as environmental pollutants.

[0003] Prior attempts to utilize water injection, ultrasonic misting, or plasma-assisted fuel reforming suffer from three primary engineering limitations:
1. High parasitic electrical consumption required to overcome the surface tension and latent heat of vaporization of ambient-temperature water;
2. Rapid cavitation erosion and boundary-layer fouling within fluidic nozzles over extended operating durations; and
3. Thermal demagnetization of high-energy-density permanent magnets (such as N52H neodymium-iron-boron) when coupled to high-compression, high-temperature reciprocating reaction chambers.

[0004] Accordingly, there exists a need for an integrated, self-regulating closed-loop architecture capable of harvesting engine and exhaust waste heat to preheat and cavitate water, utilizing nanosecond high-voltage corona discharge to dissociate water and atomize airborne particulates, and converting thermal, kinetic, acoustic, and ionic gradients into regulated dual-bus electrical power over an operational lifespan exceeding 25,000 hours.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
[0005] The present invention addresses the foregoing challenges through a synergistic closed-loop thermo-chemical and electro-mechanical system ("Cool Power Core") designed by Thomas G. Coleman.

[0006] In one aspect, exhaust gas from a 50 Hz opposed-piston reaction core is bifurcated by automated fluidic valves controlled by a 15 W Electronic Control Unit (ECU). A high-temperature exhaust fraction is geometrically shaped into a tangential control jet entering a CVD graphene-coated fluidic vortex amplifier, passively inducing low-pressure hydrodynamic cavitation alongside a 30 W (nominal) ultrasonic transducer. A second exhaust fraction transfers thermal energy into a vacuum-insulated water reservoir maintained at 90°C, offloading 43.93 W of sensible heating at a 0.54 L/hr (0.15 g/s) water feed rate and reducing electrical cavitation power demand to approximately 16.5 W–21 W.

[0007] In another aspect, the 90°C cavitated cold steam—laden with hydrogen and oxygen radicals, ambient air, or urban smog particulates—passes through a 33.6 kV corona and ozone (O₃) arc gap energized by a 4.2 V, 20 W ZVS nanosecond pulser driving a 1000× turns-ratio MnZn ferrite U-core transformer and an 8-stage Cockcroft-Walton voltage multiplier. The nanosecond plasma discharge cracks water vapor into reactive H• and OH• species while atomizing and oxidizing airborne carbonaceous particulates.

[0008] In yet another aspect, the reactive charge is compressed at a 30:1 ratio inside a 25 mm bore × 40 mm stroke opposed-piston chamber lined with yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramic and supported on 5-micron porous air bearings. An argon-filled silica aerogel thermal barrier isolates the ~3,000°C reaction zone from radial-magnetized N52H permanent magnet linear alternators, maintaining magnet operating temperatures below 100°C over a 25,000-hour minimum design life.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### A. Waste-Heat Preheating & Graphene Fluidic Vortex Cavitation
[0009] Referring to FIG. 1 and FIG. 3, a vacuum-insulated water reservoir stores recovered exhaust condensate and maintains the working fluid at a regulated setpoint of **90°C ± 1.5°C** using waste heat harvested from the engine block cooling jacket and low-temperature exhaust heat exchanger. At a nominal metered water feed rate of **0.54 liters/hour (0.15 g/s)**, raising liquid water from 20°C to 90°C recovers **43.93 W** of continuous sensible heat (\(P = \dot{m} \cdot c_p \cdot \Delta T\)).

[0010] Heating the feedwater to 90°C elevates its saturated vapor pressure from 2.3 kPa to **70.1 kPa** and reduces liquid surface tension by approximately 17%. When injected into the **CVD graphene-coated fluidic vortex amplifier (`310`)**, the 90°C water requires only **16.5 W to 21.0 W** of ultrasonic transducer (`316`) input (down from 30 W nominal for cold water) to achieve full cavitation. Simultaneously, automated fluidic valves divert a shaped high-temperature exhaust jet (`312`) tangentially into the vortex control port, establishing a low-pressure vortex core that atomizes the preheated liquid into radical-rich cold steam without moving mechanical valves. A multi-layer chemical vapor deposition (CVD) graphene coating on all wetted fluidic channels lowers boundary-layer shear drag and prevents cavitation pitting across a minimum **25,000-hour** service life.

### B. 33.6 kV Nanosecond ZVS Corona & Ozone Plasma Stage
[0011] The cavitated cold steam stream mixes with ambient intake air or urban smog and enters the plasma reaction zone (`308`). A **4.2 V, 20 W zero-voltage-switching (ZVS) nanosecond pulser (`302`)** drives the primary winding of a **1000× turns-ratio PC95 MnZn ferrite U-core transformer (`304`)**, stepping the pulse potential up to an **8-stage Cockcroft-Walton voltage multiplier (`306`)** to produce a **33.6 kV** corona and ozone (O₃) arc discharge across precision-gapped thoriated tungsten electrodes.

[0012] Nanosecond pulse modulation (pulse width < 50 ns) prevents thermal arc transition while maximizing electron-impact dissociation of H₂O into H•, O•, and OH• radicals, generating localized O₃, and electrostatically atomizing airborne soot, VOCs, and carbonaceous particulates into reactive gaseous fragments.

### C. 50 Hz Opposed-Piston Core & Argon Aerogel Magnetic Isolation
[0013] Referring to FIG. 2, the plasma-activated mixture enters a **25 mm bore × 40 mm stroke** opposed-piston combustion chamber (`202`) lined with **8 mol% yttria-stabilized zirconia (8YSZ)** ceramic. Two opposed pistons (`204A, 204B`) reciprocate out of phase at **50 Hz (3,000 cycles/min)** on **5.0 ± 0.5 µm** porous carbon-graphite air bearings (`206A, 206B`), achieving a **30:1 geometric compression ratio** and peak reaction temperatures approaching **3,000°C** without liquid lubricants.

[0014] Each piston shaft carries a **radial-magnetized N52H neodymium-iron-boron permanent magnet array (`210A, 210B`)** reciprocating linearly within multi-phase copper stator coils (`212A, 212B`) to directly generate electrical power. Because N52H magnets suffer irreversible flux loss above 120°C, an **8 mm radial thermal barrier (`208`)** composed of argon-filled hydrophobic silica aerogel (\(k pprox 0.014	ext{ W/m}\cdot	ext{K}\)) surrounds the 8YSZ liner (`202`), maintaining the N52H magnet arrays below **100°C** during continuous 3,000°C core operation.

### D. Multi-Modal Exhaust Harvesting & Closed-Loop Water/Oxygen Recovery
[0015] High-enthalpy exhaust gases exiting manifold (`216`) pass sequentially through:
1. A **Variable Geometry Turbocharger (VGT)** exhaust loop regulating backpressure and scavenging;
2. A **biomimetic micro Tesla boundary-layer steam turbine** coupled to a high-speed micro-alternator;
3. An **electrostatic ion grid** harvesting charged plasma species;
4. High-temperature **bismuth titanate piezoelectric (PZT) pressure harvesters** capturing 50 Hz acoustic pressure pulses;
5. A hybrid **thermoelectric generator (TEG) and pyroelectric thermal stack** mounted across the engine block and exhaust jacket; and
6. A **vanadium pentoxide / titanium dioxide (V₂O₅/TiO₂) Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) honeycomb monolith** neutralizing residual \(	ext{NO}_x\) and \(	ext{SO}_x\).

[0016] Downstream of the SCR catalyst, a condenser cools the exhaust stream below its dew point to recover liquid water back into the 90°C thermal reservoir, while separating excess oxygen and venting scrubbed exhaust gas. A **15 W ARM Cortex-M7 ECU** dynamically modulates the automated fluidic valves and water injection rate to maintain optimal intake humidity for plasma cracking and regulates a split high-voltage (48 V) / low-voltage (12 V / 4.2 V) electrical power bus.

## CLAIMS

**What is claimed is:**

**1.** A closed-loop thermo-chemical and electro-mechanical energy conversion system, comprising:
* (a) an insulated water reservoir configured to store recovered water and maintain said water at approximately 90°C using waste heat harvested from an engine block and an exhaust loop;
* (b) a chemical-vapor-deposition (CVD) graphene-coated fluidic vortex amplifier fluidly coupled to said water reservoir and an intake air stream, configured to cavitate said preheated water into radical-rich cold steam using a shaped high-temperature exhaust control stream and ultrasonic excitation;
* (c) a nanosecond-pulsed high-voltage plasma stage comprising a 4.2 V, 20 W zero-voltage-switching (ZVS) driver, a 1000× MnZn ferrite U-core transformer, and an 8-stage Cockcroft-Walton voltage multiplier configured to apply a 33.6 kV corona and ozone discharge across said cavitated cold steam and intake air stream;
* (d) a 50 Hz opposed-piston reaction core having a 25 mm bore, a 40 mm stroke, a 30:1 compression ratio, a yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) ceramic liner, 5-micron porous air bearings, and an argon-filled silica aerogel thermal barrier isolating a 3,000°C reaction chamber from radial-magnetized N52H linear alternators;
* (e) a multi-modal exhaust energy recovery train comprising a Variable Geometry Turbocharger (VGT) exhaust loop, a biomimetic micro Tesla steam turbine, an electrostatic ion grid, piezoelectric (PZT) exhaust pressure harvesters, a thermoelectric and pyroelectric thermal recovery stack, and a vanadium/titanium dioxide (V₂O₅/TiO₂) Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) honeycomb block; and
* (f) a 15 W Electronic Control Unit (ECU) and automated fluidic valves configured to bifurcate exhaust flow into high-temperature and low-temperature streams, meter water injection to maintain optimum humidity for plasma cracking, and regulate a split high/low voltage electrical output bus.

**2.** The system of claim 1, wherein preheating the water feed rate of 0.54 liters/hour from 20°C to 90°C in the thermal reservoir recovers approximately 43.93 W of sensible waste heat and reduces electrical cavitation power demand by 30% to 45% relative to ambient-temperature cavitation.

**3.** The system of claim 1, wherein the CVD graphene coating applied to wetted surfaces of the fluidic vortex amplifier prevents cavitation erosion and boundary-layer fouling over a minimum operational runtime of 25,000 hours.

**4.** The system of claim 1, wherein the automated fluidic valves route the high-temperature exhaust stream through a converging-diverging nozzle into a tangential control port of the fluidic vortex amplifier to passively induce hydrodynamic cavitation without moving mechanical valves in the hot gas path.

**5.** The system of claim 1, wherein the argon-filled silica aerogel thermal barrier surrounds the YSZ ceramic liner to maintain the radial-magnetized N52H permanent magnets below 100°C while the opposed-piston combustion chamber operates at peak temperatures up to 3,000°C.

**6.** The system of claim 1, wherein the intake air stream draws urban smog containing volatile organic compounds and carbonaceous particulates, and wherein the 33.6 kV corona and ozone discharge atomizes and oxidizes said particulates prior to 30:1 compression in the opposed-piston reaction core.

**7.** A method of operating a closed-loop energy conversion system, comprising the steps of:
* (a) preheating water in a thermal storage reservoir to 90°C using waste heat from an opposed-piston engine block and exhaust stream;
* (b) injecting said 90°C water into a graphene-coated fluidic vortex cavitator driven by a shaped high-temperature exhaust jet to generate radical-rich cold steam at controlled intake humidity;
* (c) energizing a 4.2 V, 20 W ZVS nanosecond pulser coupled to a 1000× MnZn ferrite U-core transformer and an 8-stage Cockcroft-Walton multiplier to generate a 33.6 kV corona and ozone discharge that dissociates water vapor and atomizes airborne particulates;
* (d) compressing and reacting the resulting radicalized mixture at 50 Hz and a 30:1 compression ratio within a YSZ-lined opposed-piston chamber supported on 5-micron air bearings while shielding radial-magnetized N52H linear alternators with an argon silica aerogel barrier;
* (e) harvesting residual kinetic, thermal, acoustic, and ionic exhaust energy via a VGT loop, micro Tesla turbine, electrostatic ion grid, PZT pressure harvesters, and TEG/pyroelectric stack before scrubbing exhaust gases across a V₂O₅/TiO₂ SCR honeycomb catalyst; and
* (f) condensing exhaust water vapor back into the 90°C thermal reservoir while outputting clean water, oxygen, and regulated dual-bus electrical power.
