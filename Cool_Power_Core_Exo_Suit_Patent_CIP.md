**Title:** METABOLIC-HEAT-ASSISTED CLOSED-LOOP LIFE-SUPPORT AND POWERED EXOSKELETON SYSTEM

## ABSTRACT
A self-powered, closed-loop life-support suit and multi-axis powered exoskeleton system (`CPC-EXO-EXT-II`) driven by a wearable micro-thermodynamic and nanosecond-plasma core. Operator metabolic waste heat captured via a liquid cooling garment (LCG) preheats a wastewater reservoir to approximately 90°C, reducing the cavitation and dissociation energy required to process metabolic urine, perspiration, fecal organics, and exhaled carbon dioxide within a graphene-coated fluidic vortex cavitator and 33.6 kV nanosecond pulsed plasma reactor. Oxygen ions separated across an 8YSZ ceramic membrane replenish breathable oxygen, while linear alternators, thermoelectric/pyroelectric harvesters, and regenerative quasi-direct-drive (QDD) joint actuators charge a graphene supercapacitor bus powering lower- and upper-body exoskeleton limbs and auxiliary electrical/fluidic power take-off (PTO) ports.

## SPECIFICATION (EXCERPTED CONTINUATION PARAGRAPHS)
**[0081]** This Continuation-in-Part expands upon parent application `CPC-PAT-2026-001` by integrating a miniaturized dual-core assembly (`µCPC-SUIT`, combined mass ≤ 3.70 kg) directly into an articulated, ground-shunted powered exoskeleton and sealed environmental suit architecture.  
**[0082]** During active human locomotion and payload carriage, metabolic heat dissipation (165 Wth to 285 Wth) is harvested by a closed-loop Liquid Cooling Garment (LCG) and transferred into a vacuum-insulated wastewater reservoir holding collected urine, sweat condensate, and greywater at 90°C ± 1.5°C, offsetting sensible preheating enthalpy and lowering acoustic/vortex cavitation threshold energy by up to 42%.  
**[0083]** A 10-axis quasi-direct-drive (QDD) brushless actuator network spanning hip, knee, lumbar, shoulder, and elbow joints is coupled to a 48 V DC bus buffered by a 180 Wh graphene supercapacitor bank. Eccentric joint motion during descent or payload lowering operates the QDD actuators in four-quadrant regenerative braking mode, returning 18.5 W to 48.0 W directly to the 48 V bus alongside +163.0 W continuous generation from the dual micro-cores.  
**[0084]** Dual auxiliary Power Take-Off (PTO) interfaces—comprising an isolated 48 V / 650 W burst electrical port and a 1.2 MPa sterile breathing-air / 90°C ozonated plasma-activated water (PAW) fluidic port—are gated by a dual-lockstep ARM Cortex-M7 supervisor that prioritizes breathable oxygen partial pressure (≥ 19.5 kPa) and N52H magnet thermal limits (< 95°C) prior to authorizing external tool discharge.

## SUPPLEMENTAL CLAIMS (CLAIMS 14–19)
**14. (Independent System Claim)** A self-powered wearable life-support and exoskeleton system, comprising:
  (a) a sealed protective suit worn by a human operator and incorporating a liquid cooling garment (LCG) configured to capture metabolic thermal energy from the operator;
  (b) a wastewater reservoir thermally coupled to the LCG to maintain a mixture of human metabolic liquid byproducts at a preheated temperature of at least 85°C;
  (c) a graphene-coated fluidic vortex cavitator and a 33.6 kV nanosecond pulsed plasma reactor configured to cavitate and dissociate the preheated metabolic byproducts and exhaled carbon dioxide;
  (d) an yttria-stabilized zirconia (8YSZ) oxygen-ion separator and Sabatier-pyrolysis loop configured to output breathable oxygen and sterile potable water back to the sealed protective suit while sequestering solid graphitic carbon;
  (e) at least one opposed-piston linear alternator and thermoelectric/pyroelectric generator stack converting thermal and pressure cycles from the reactor into electrical power on a direct-current (DC) bus; and
  (f) a multi-joint articulated exoskeleton frame coupled to the DC bus and comprising quasi-direct-drive (QDD) brushless actuators configured to assist operator limb movement and regenerate electrical power during eccentric joint deceleration.

**15. (Dependent Claim)** The system of claim 14, wherein the DC bus comprises a 48 V primary rail buffered by a graphene supercapacitor bank having a specific energy of at least 95 Wh/kg to supply peak actuation bursts exceeding 380 W without depleting life-support power reserves.

**16. (Dependent Claim)** The system of claim 14, further comprising an auxiliary electrical power take-off (PTO) port and a fluidic PTO port supplying pressurized sterile air or 90°C ozonated plasma-activated water (PAW) to external handheld tools, gated by a real-time controller that disables PTO output whenever suit oxygen partial pressure drops below 19.5 kPa.

**17. (Dependent Claim)** The system of claim 14, wherein the QDD brushless actuators comprise 6-axis force/torque cuff sensors operating under a 1 kHz human-intent admittance loop nested over a 10 kHz field-oriented control (FOC) current loop.

**18. (Dependent Claim)** The system of claim 14, wherein an ambient intake scrubber routes hazardous chemical, biological, radiological, or nuclear (CBRN) particulates through the 33.6 kV nanosecond pulsed plasma reactor to achieve at least 6-log pathogen and volatile organic compound destruction prior to cabin breathing-air introduction.

**19. (Independent Method Claim)** A method of sustaining a human operator in an isolated hazardous environment using a powered exoskeleton, comprising:
  (a) harvesting metabolic body heat from the operator via a liquid cooling garment to preheat collected human wastewater to approximately 90°C;
  (b) cavitating and plasma-cracking the preheated wastewater and exhaled carbon dioxide to generate breathable oxygen, distilled water, and continuous electrical power;
  (c) storing surplus electrical energy in a graphene supercapacitor bank; and
  (d) powering multi-axis exoskeleton actuators from the supercapacitor bank while feeding regenerative braking energy from eccentric limb motion back into the electrical bus.
