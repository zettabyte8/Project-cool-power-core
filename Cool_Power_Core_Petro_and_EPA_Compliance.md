## Part I: Petrochemical Sour-Water & $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ Plasma-Cracking Model (`CPC-PETRO`)

### 1. Thermodynamic Advantage Over Conventional Claus Sulfur Recovery
Conventional refinery Amine Treating + Claus Sulfur Recovery Units (SRUs) burn one-third of the hydrogen sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) to $\text{SO}_2$ just to drive thermal oxidation—destroying valuable hydrogen into low-grade steam while still requiring a Tail Gas Treating Unit (TGTU).

The **Cool Power Core (`CPC-PETRO`)** directly dissociates $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ and dissolved refinery sour-water contaminants (phenols, ammonia, mercaptans, benzene) via **90°C CVD graphene vortex cavitation** and **33.6 kV ZVS nanosecond non-thermal plasma**:

$$2\text{H}_2\text{S} \xrightarrow{\text{33.6 kV ZVS Plasma + 90°C Cavitation}} 2\text{H}_2\uparrow + \text{S}_2(\text{solid/liquid})\downarrow \quad (\Delta H^\circ = +20.6\text{ kJ/mol } \text{H}_2\text{S})$$

* **8.5× Lower Dissociation Enthalpy vs. Water:** Splitting $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ requires only **20.6 kJ/mol** compared to **285.8 kJ/mol** for $\text{H}_2\text{O}$—allowing the 33.6 kV nanosecond streamer discharge to achieve **>99.95% $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ conversion** in a single pass.
* **Refinery Waste-Heat Offloading:** Routing **115°C–160°C refinery stripper overhead vapor or hot condensate** into the reservoir offloads **43.93 W/core** sensible preheating and **339.0 W/core** latent vaporization enthalpy, dropping CVD graphene vortex cavitation power from **30.0 W** to **14.8 W**.
* **Multi-Product Valorization (Per 1,000-Core ISO Skid):**
  * **Net Continuous Electricity:** **+338.2 kW** (+338.2 W/core) exported to refinery microgrid.
  * **Bright Elemental Sulfur ($\text{S}_8$):** **395.0 kg/hr** (9.48 MT/day) condensed at 135°C in the cyclone separator.
  * **High-Purity Green/Turquoise Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$):** **24.8 kg/hr** (595.2 kg/day) recovered via Pd-Ag membrane slipstream (or co-combusted in the 3,000°C opposed-piston core for maximum shaft/electric power).
  * **Stripped Distilled Boiler Feedwater:** **518.4 L/hr** (12,442 L/day, `<2 ppm` sulfides/ammonia) returned directly to refinery boilers.
